# Homepage: the text above the four-step progression in "From gene discovery to Geneial".
---
My PhD analysis flagged 154 candidate disease genes. A candidate gene is a lead, not a diagnosis. Confirming one takes more patients, more data, and often years, and in the meantime the families, datasets, and researchers who could close the gap tend to drift apart. Data collected for one study rarely makes it to the next, and people who contributed often never hear what came of it.

Geneial grew out of that problem. Its first proof of concept was built with the lab where I trained: privacy-preserving search of the Xia-Gibbs syndrome registry at Baylor’s Human Genome Sequencing Center. I had contributed to Xia-Gibbs research there as a graduate student. That community’s registry now runs on Geneial.

I think research infrastructure should make it easier for patients to stay connected to the science their data enables, and researchers should be able to learn from longitudinal data without treating people as one-time data sources.
