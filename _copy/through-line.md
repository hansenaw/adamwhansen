# Homepage: the text above the four-step progression in "From gene discovery to Geneial".
---
My mother is a nurse and my father is an inventor. I deliberately chose a career that combines the two: using new ideas to relieve suffering. From my father’s work, I learned that a good idea is only the beginning. Getting it to the people it could help is the hard part. So I trained across genetics, entrepreneurship, and healthcare to learn how that happens.

My PhD analysis flagged 154 candidate disease genes. Often we couldn’t move them forward: the clinical picture was incomplete, and we had no reliable way to reconnect with families as new questions came up. Most of what we found never made it back to the families who had contributed their data.

That was the problem I’d been preparing to solve. Geneial’s first proof of concept was built with the lab where I trained: privacy-preserving search of the Xia-Gibbs syndrome registry at Baylor’s Human Genome Sequencing Center, a community whose research I’d contributed to as a graduate student. That registry now runs on Geneial.
